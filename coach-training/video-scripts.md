## Video 2 — Child Safety First (≈2 min)

**On screen title:** "Safety Is Everything"

> Before anything else — safety. This is the part there's no flexibility on.
>
> One: you must have a current Blue Card before you work with our kids. No exceptions. If yours is close to expiring, tell me straight away.
>
> Two: never be alone, out of sight, one-on-one with a child. Stay where you can be seen. It protects them and it protects you.
>
> Three: you're responsible for every child in your room until they're collected by someone authorised. The tablet tells you who's allowed to pick each child up — check it.
>
> And four: if anything ever worries you — a bruise, something a child says, anything at all — you tell me the same day. Not next week. The same day.
>
> Get safety right and everything else is fun. Let's keep going.

## Video 3 — Running the Room (≈3 min)

**On screen title:** "How We Run a Class"

> Okay, the secret to a great class? Structure. Chaos doesn't come from bad kids — it comes from dead time. So we kill the dead time.
>
> Use stations. Small groups, rotating through activities, everyone always doing something — nobody standing in a line waiting for a turn. Lines are where it all falls apart.
>
> Same routine every week. Same opening circle, same warm-up, same way we finish. Kids feel safe when they know what's coming.
>
> Burn the energy first. Start with five minutes of big movement — running, jumping, the trampoline — then teach the skill. Never the other way around.
>
> Three rules, and we say them as DOs, not don'ts: safe body, listening ears, have a go. That's it.
>
> And warn them before every change: "two more goes… five, four, three, two, one, swap." No cold stops.
>
> Nail this and you've nailed coaching.

## Video 4 — Managing Big Energy, ADHD & Boundary-Testers (≈3 min)

**On screen title:** "Big Energy Kids"

> Some of our best kids come in with massive energy — and a lot of them have ADHD or are a bit neurodiverse. Here's the truth: circus is one of the best things on earth for these kids. So we don't see them as a problem. We see them as ours to help.
>
> The golden rule: prevent, don't punish. Set the room up so it goes well — busy stations, clear expectations, no waiting.
>
> Catch them being good, instantly. The second a kid does something right, you tell them — "great pointed toes, Jack!" — and you award a star right there. That instant reward is exactly what these kids' brains need.
>
> Give the live-wire a job. Make them your demonstrator, your equipment helper. Channel that energy into responsibility.
>
> Get close and go quiet — standing next to a kid beats shouting across the room every time.
>
> And when it tips over — a meltdown, a flat refusal — you stay calm. Your calm is contagious. Shrink the audience, give them a choice not a battle, and use a reset corner for sixty seconds. That's a break, not a punishment.
>
> Oh — and check the tablet before class. If a child has a support note, it'll be right there: "give him a job, warn before transitions." Read it. It's gold.

## Video 5 — The Star Rewards System (≈2 min)

**On screen title:** "Stars & Rewards"

> Our Star Rewards system is the heartbeat of the place, and you're the one handing out the magic.
>
> Award stars for effort and milestones — not just for being the best. The kid who finally got the courage to try the silks? Star. The kid who helped pack up? Star.
>
> Do it live and do it specific. "I'm giving you a star for never giving up on that cartwheel." They remember those moments.
>
> Stars build up, kids climb the tiers, and it keeps them coming back excited. Be generous but genuine — kids can smell a fake star a mile off. Use it well and you'll see them light up.

## Video 6 — Roll Call, StarBand & the Tablet (≈2 min)

**On screen title:** "The Tablet & Roll Call"

> Every class runs through the tablet, and it does three big jobs.
>
> One — the roll. Mark every child in. This isn't admin, it's safety: it's our register if we ever have to evacuate the building.
>
> Two — the flags. Before class, glance at each child's card. Allergies show in red, medical notes too, and support needs show with a little brain icon. Ten seconds of looking can save a kid's day.
>
> Three — the stars. Award them right on the tablet as you go.
>
> The kids tap in with their StarBand wristband, you confirm the room on your screen, and you're set. Easy.

## Video 7 — Your Circus Skills (≈2 min)

**On screen title:** "Learning the Circus Craft"

> Now — you came to us with great foundations: acro, tumbling, body awareness. What we're going to teach you is the circus layer, and this is the fun part.
>
> We build it in order. Acro and tumbling you've got. Then aerial — silks, lyra, trapeze. Then juggling and object work. Then the performance side — clowning, character, putting on a show.
>
> Two important things. One: you don't teach aerial until you're signed off on spotting and rigging safety. That's non-negotiable. Two: every new skill you master and get signed off to teach, you earn more. Your skills grow, your pay grows. Simple as that.
>
> Take it seriously, have fun with it, and you'll become a genuine all-rounder.
