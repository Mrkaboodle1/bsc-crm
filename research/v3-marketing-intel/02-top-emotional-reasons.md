# 02 · Top Emotional Reasons Parents Enroll Their Kids

*Why parents say YES, ranked by frequency in 2024–2026 Australian data. Skill or talent isn't in the top five.*

The headline finding from the [Australian Sports Commission Clearinghouse](https://www.ausport.gov.au/clearinghouse/evidence/children-and-youth-in-sport/factors/parents): **44% of parents enrol because "my child wanted to play"**; **27% because "friends were joining"**. Two-thirds of the decision is emotional and social, not athletic.

## The ranked list

### 1. 🤝 "My child wants to feel like they belong somewhere"

The #1 emotional driver, per the [Allianz 2024 youth-sport study](https://www.allianz.com.au/about-us/media-hub/aussie-kids-drop-out-of-sport.html): **88% of parents cite cheered-on / affirmation / supported as the top motivator** their child gets from an activity. Belonging beats winning. Beats skill. Beats fitness.

**BSC angle:** "Where every kid finds their place" isn't a tagline — it's the literal #1 reason parents enrol kids in *anything*.

---

### 2. 💪 "I want them to be more confident"

The Triple P 2024 National Parenting Survey shows **9 in 10 Australian parents are operating under elevated cost-of-living stress**, with parental guilt cycles peaking weekly. Confidence in their child is the antidote they're chasing.

Peer-reviewed evidence specifically supports circus on this dimension: the [Stevens et al. 2023 PMC scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10003779/) found **23 of 57 reviewed circus interventions documented measurable improvements in confidence and self-esteem**. The University of South Australia [Cirkidz Tweenz study](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2019/05/190509101843.htm) of 55 kids aged 8–14 documented significant lifts in stress relief, self-esteem, confidence, and socialisation.

**BSC angle:** Confidence is the deliverable. Cite the PMC review when needed — it's peer-reviewed gold.

---

### 3. 📵 "Get them off screens"

[ABS data](https://www.abs.gov.au/articles/kids-clock-10-or-more-hours-screen-time-week): **~90% of Australian kids exceed 10 hours of weekly screen time**. The under-16 social media ban (10 Dec 2025, per [eSafety](https://www.esafety.gov.au/parents/issues-and-advice/screen-time)) made screens a government-recognised problem. Parents now have **state-level permission to feel justified in pulling kids off screens** — they're hunting for the activity that earns the swap.

[2025 nature/screen-balance research (PMC12752391)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12752391/) finds parents describe immersive offline play as **"relief from screen-related parenting challenges and associated guilt"** — meaning the sale is guilt-relief, not just wellness.

**BSC angle:** "Screen-free for 60 minutes. Your kid won't even ask for the iPad on the drive home." Show, don't claim — coach intros and class-floor B-roll do this work.

---

### 4. 👯 "They need to make friends"

[Aus Sports Commission](https://www.ausport.gov.au/clearinghouse/evidence/children-and-youth-in-sport/factors/parents): **27% of parents enrol because "friends were joining"**. Among older kids (10–14), peer pressure becomes the dominant entry vector.

Bedroom isolation post-COVID compounds this — the Headspace 2025 release shows **49% of young Australians report high or very high psychological distress**, with social-skill deficits a common adolescent presentation ([Headspace](https://headspace.org.au/our-organisation/media-releases/nearly-half-of-young-australians-experiencing-high-levels-of-psychological-distress-but-more-are-seeking-support/)).

**BSC angle:** Group-class structure + small class sizes = a contained, supervised friendship-formation environment. The "first time I made a friend at circus" testimonial is gold.

---

### 5. 🧠 "Get the energy out — for their mental health"

[AIHW data via Young Minds Matter](https://youngmindsmatter.thekids.org.au/our-research/prevalence-of-mental-disorders/): **13.9% of Australian kids 4–17 meet criteria for a mental disorder**. ADHD is 7.4% (the #1 boys' disorder); anxiety 6.9% (the #1 girls' disorder). Parents of kids with diagnosis or signs of either are **actively seeking** physical activity as part of the management plan.

The connection between physical activity and child mental health is well-evidenced in the AIHW reports; what's *new* in 2026 is parental awareness — Triple P and Headspace coverage has put mental-health-via-movement into mainstream parenting conversation.

**BSC angle:** Don't medicalise the language. Just show the kid coming OFF the mats glowing, sweat-soaked, smiling, dopamine-spent. Subtle but unmistakable.

---

### 6. 🎯 "They're searching for their thing"

Many parents enrol almost diagnostically — they want to find out *what* their kid is. Especially true for parents of:
- Quiet/shy kids who don't fit team sports
- Neurodiverse kids who've washed out of one or two activities
- Creative kids who feel out of place at school

This is **why multi-discipline matters**: BSC's acro + aerial + drama + fusion structure means a kid can rotate within one studio rather than requiring the parent to start over at a new school. Single-discipline operators lose this audience.

---

### 7. 💕 "I want them to have a memory of being a kid"

Underrated. Triple P research shows **parents feel guilty about "not doing enough" with their kid**. Enrolling in an activity is a **purchased memory**. End-of-term parent showcases, grandparent-watching moments, the photo on the silks — these aren't perks, they're the product.

---

### 8. 🚸 "Safety for an hour"

Less romantic but real. Working parents need **supervised hours**. Activities are also childcare. This sits underneath every enrolment decision but parents rarely admit it.

**BSC angle:** Don't say it out loud, but make the schedule fit school-pickup + work-pickup windows. Sat 9am, weekday 3:30–5pm, school holiday whole-day = peak utility.

---

### 9. 💜 "Inclusion — for kids who don't fit anywhere else"

NDIS / Thriving Kids parents are explicitly searching for inclusive activities. Per the [UWA April 2026 article](https://www.uwa.edu.au/news/article/2026/april/autism-diagnoses-are-up-largely-fuelled-by-ndis-what-happens-next-isnt-clear), autism diagnosis rates are rising fast, and families are actively seeking inclusive, non-clinical activities for these kids.

This is BSC's **largest moat against generic gymnastics**: explicit neurodivergent welcoming language. The PMC review's specific autism/dev-delay finding is a credible foundation.

---

### 10. 💸 "I have a Play On voucher and it expires soon"

Not emotional — financial. But it's the **single biggest active acquisition lever in QLD right now**: every kid 5–17 has $200 of state money. [Round 2 closes 12 June 2026](https://www.qld.gov.au/recreation/sports/funding/playon). Cirkidz Adelaide is already running voucher-tied Meta creative (Library ID 2061288291101437). No GC operator is. Take this hill.
