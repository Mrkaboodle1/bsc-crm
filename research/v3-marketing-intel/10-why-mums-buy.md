# 10 · "Why Mums Buy" — Emotional Trigger Report

*The psychology of the Australian mum's purchase decision. If you read one doc in this folder, make it this one.*

## The headline insight

Per the [Mi3 / Mumpower 2025 Aussie Mums Report](https://www.mi-3.com.au/23-04-2025/trust-and-reviews-shape-australian-mums-buying-decisions-reveals-new-report):

- **1 in 4** Australian mums say their most recent purchase was triggered by **a recommendation from someone they trust**
- **50%** consult **other mothers** before purchasing
- **6 in 10** actively **promote brands they favour** online

**Translation:** Mums in 2026 don't make purchase decisions alone. They make them in a network. The mum you sell to becomes a salesperson for you to the next three mums. Once you're in, you compound.

## The 6-stage mum buying journey (for a kids' activity)

This is what's actually happening inside the mum's head between "first sees you" and "books the trial":

### Stage 1 — Awareness flicker (Day 0)

She sees BSC. Maybe a Reel from a mum she trusts. Maybe a school newsletter mention. Maybe a comment in the Gold Coast Mums Group. **She does not click yet.** She files it: *"This place looks interesting."*

### Stage 2 — Background research (Day 0-3)

She visits the website briefly (~90 seconds). Looks at Instagram for ~12 minutes total spread across multiple visits. Checks Google reviews. **This is where 60% of leads silently die** — the website or the Instagram contradicted what she initially felt.

### Stage 3 — Trust signal hunt (Day 1-5)

She's looking for unconscious signals:
- Other mums in the comments / reviews
- Coach faces (not just kid stunts)
- Blue Card, first aid, insurance mentioned
- Photos of NORMAL kids, not just the prodigies
- Pricing transparency
- "Free trial" without a card-on-file requirement

### Stage 4 — Social validation (Day 2-7)

She asks. Either publicly in a mum group, or privately to one specific friend whose taste she trusts. This is **the moment of truth** — if no one in her network has a positive thing to say, BSC is dead.

### Stage 5 — Soft commitment (Day 5-10)

She DMs BSC (rarely calls) with a softball question. *"Hi, what age does your beginner aerial class start?"* She's not really asking. She's **feeling out** whether the business is warm and responsive. **Reply within 60 minutes = converted. Reply at 4pm tomorrow = dead.**

### Stage 6 — Trial booking (Day 7-14)

She books. Usually a free trial, on a Saturday morning, with one or both kids. She brings a friend if she can. **The trial is now the entire sale** — see doc 15.

## What activates each stage (with research backing)

### Activator for Stage 1 (Awareness)

**Other mums** doing the talking. Per the [Australian Sports Commission](https://www.ausport.gov.au/clearinghouse/evidence/children-and-youth-in-sport/factors/parents) — **27% of parents enrol because "friends were joining"**. Per Mi3 — 50% consult other mothers first.

This is why **user-generated content** outperforms produced creative in family niches. A Reel of a real BSC mum saying *"my daughter has been a different kid since starting"* hits a stage-1 lever no polished commercial can match.

### Activator for Stage 2 (Background research)

**Authenticity over polish**. A Reel of a coach in a slightly messy studio explaining how a class works → converts. A drone-shot promo with corporate voiceover → does not. Per [Adligator's 2026 creative trends](https://adligator.com/blog/facebook-ad-creative-trends-2026), UGC-style assets have 8.7× higher engagement.

### Activator for Stage 3 (Trust signal hunt)

The unconscious checklist:

- ✅ **Coach bios** with faces + Blue Card numbers + first-aid certs
- ✅ **Real photos of normal kids** (not curated elite-performer shots)
- ✅ **Pricing in dollars** on the website, not "contact us for pricing"
- ✅ **Google reviews** (4.8+ stars, multiple recent)
- ✅ **Free trial** with no card required

See doc 14 for the full trust framework.

### Activator for Stage 4 (Social validation)

**Be in the rooms where she asks**. The big ones for Gold Coast:

- Gold Coast Mums Group (FB, ~15K members)
- The class parent WhatsApp groups (BSC can't be in, but can ASK parents to talk about us)
- The school P&C newsletter blurb
- The Kids on the Coast feature

### Activator for Stage 5 (Soft commitment / DM)

**Reply velocity matters more than reply quality**. Per industry data on chat conversion: a reply within 15 minutes converts ~7× higher than a reply within 24 hours. Jacky's role here is decisive — drafted replies waiting for Rhett's tap during work hours = sub-1-hour median response time, the kind of velocity no competitor with manual-only ops can match.

### Activator for Stage 6 (Trial)

The mum already wants to enrol. The trial isn't a sale — it's an excuse not to buy. Things that *kill* a trial after booking:

- Confusing arrival instructions
- Coach who doesn't know the kid's name
- Other parents who are clique-y
- Kid having a bad moment with no recovery
- Awkward exit conversation

See doc 15 for the full trial choreography.

## The 8 emotional triggers that move money

Ranked roughly by power for the BSC audience. Each pairs a *core emotion* with a *language pattern*.

| # | Emotion | Language pattern | When to use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Belonging** | "Find their place / find their people" | Hero copy everywhere |
| 2 | **Pride** | "Watch their face when they get it" | Transformation Reels |
| 3 | **Relief** | "You don't have to push them" | Anxiety-named hooks |
| 4 | **Hope** | "Some kids just need the right room" | Shy-kid storytelling |
| 5 | **Trust** | "Sarah's been coaching here 9 years" | Coach intros |
| 6 | **Permission** | "It's okay to want screen-free" | Screen-time content |
| 7 | **Specificity** | "Pimpama mum, 8-year-old daughter" | Hyper-targeted ads |
| 8 | **Scarcity (gentle)** | "3 spots left in Saturday's class" | Term-launch creative |

## Triggers that BACKFIRE in 2026

| Trigger | Why it fails | Replace with |
|---|---|---|
| Hard urgency ("BOOK NOW!") | Reads as pushy | Soft scarcity ("3 spots left") |
| Fear-based ("don't let your child fall behind") | Reads as predatory | Hope-based ("help them find their thing") |
| Elite framing ("Australia's #1 / award-winning") | Intimidates the parent of a normal kid | Inclusive framing ("for every kid") |
| Discount as primary message ("20% off!") | Devalues the brand | Value-applied ("$200 voucher auto-applied") |
| Skill-claim language ("learn aerial silks") | Skill is means, not end | Outcome language ("watch their confidence grow") |
| "Limited spots remaining!!!" | Reads as artificial | Specific spot count ("3 spots in 4pm class") |

## The neurology underneath

In simplified terms, three brain systems are firing when a mum considers an activity:

1. **Threat detection** — Is this safe? (Coach faces, Blue Card, reviews)
2. **Social calibration** — Are people like me doing this? (UGC, mum-group mentions)
3. **Reward anticipation** — Will my kid be happier? (Transformation arcs, parent-watching-kid shots)

**Effective marketing fires all three.** Generic marketing fires none. Hard-sell marketing fires threat detection in the WRONG direction (this person is trying to manipulate me).

## What this means for BSC's brand voice

The Allianz 2024 study found **88% of parents say the top motivators for kids are being cheered on, positive affirmation, and feeling proud**. Mums want the BUSINESS to model the behaviour they want for their kid. Warm. Encouraging. Specific. Patient.

**The brand voice rules:**

- Speak to her like a friend who happens to run a circus, not like a marketer
- Specifics over generalities (suburb + age + behaviour > "parents of kids")
- One emotion per message — don't try to do all 8 triggers in one Reel
- Coaches and parents in their own voice, not Rhett's voice for everyone
