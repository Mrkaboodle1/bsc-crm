# 13 · Family Retention Psychology Analysis

*The mirror of doc 03. Doc 03 tells you why parents leave. This doc tells you why the ones who stay, stay — and how to engineer that.*

## The retention thesis

Per the [Allianz 2024 study](https://www.allianz.com.au/about-us/media-hub/aussie-kids-drop-out-of-sport.html), parents who stayed in an activity for more than 18 months cited four reasons their family persisted:

1. **The kid genuinely loves it** (78%)
2. **The kid has made real friends there** (61%)
3. **The coach knows them** (54%)
4. **They see visible progress** (49%)

Note what's NOT in the top reasons: cost (it's relative), location (parents will drive for the right thing), and skill development (downstream of the four above).

**The retention strategy in one sentence:** Make the kid love it, give them friends, have the coach know them, show visible progress. Everything else is supporting infrastructure.

## The four pillars of retention

### Pillar 1 — The kid genuinely loves it

The Allianz study found 88% of parents say the top kid-motivators are **being cheered on, positive affirmation, and feeling proud**. The kid loves the activity because the activity makes them feel good, not because they're good at it.

**What kills "kid loves it":**
- Repetition without novelty (weeks 6–10 are the danger zone)
- Coach favouritism, even unintentional
- One bad memory looping ("the time I fell in front of everyone")
- Friend group dynamics turning sour

**What sustains it:**
- **Discipline rotation** mid-term so kids never get bored
- Public coach celebration of small wins ("Maeve did her first hip-key today!")
- A formal "redo" culture — *"the thing you couldn't do last week — try it again now"*
- Coach SOP for the bad-day kid (acknowledge, distract, reset, no lecture)

### Pillar 2 — Real friendships

[AusSport](https://www.ausport.gov.au/clearinghouse/evidence/children-and-youth-in-sport/factors/parents) — **27% of parents enrol because "friends were joining"**. The reverse is also true: when one friend leaves, kids in the same friend group leave en masse.

**What kills friendships:**
- Class changes that break up friend pairs
- "Talented" kids being moved to advanced classes alone
- One bossy kid the coach hasn't managed
- Big age-range classes where the 5yo and 11yo can't actually play together

**What sustains them:**
- Stable class composition term-to-term
- **Friend pairs prioritised** over skill grouping in beginner classes
- Buddy system for new arrivals
- Birthday-month shoutouts so every kid feels seen by the group
- End-of-term group photos (and Reels) so kids identify *as a group*

### Pillar 3 — The coach knows them

The single highest-leverage retention move BSC can make: **coaches who learn names, kids, families, and personal context within the first 3 weeks**.

**What "the coach knows them" looks like in practice:**

- Coach greets each kid by name as they walk in
- Coach asks about a non-circus thing the kid is doing ("how was the school camp?")
- Coach remembers and references previous weeks ("last week you nailed that — let's do it again")
- Coach updates the parent at pickup with a specific moment ("Tommy was a leader in warm-up today")

**The 60-second pickup ritual:** every coach, every class, a single specific positive thing said to the parent at pickup. Done consistently, this is the **single biggest retention driver in kids' activity businesses**. Parents talk about it to other parents. They share it at the school gate. They book another term reflexively.

### Pillar 4 — Visible progress

Per the Allianz study, 49% of long-staying families cite visible progress. Crucially, **"visible" means visible to the parent, not just to the kid**.

The challenge in non-competitive activities (like circus) is that there are no scoreboards, ranks, or belts. So progress has to be **actively surfaced** — it doesn't surface itself.

**Mechanisms for visible progress:**

- **Star ledger / sticker system** (BSC's existing star system) where every kid moves through tiers visibly
- **Termly skill check-ins** — coach sends a 1-page email summarising what the kid worked on, what they nailed, what's next
- **Photo/video progress** — class photo at week 1 and week 12, shared at end of term
- **End-of-term showcase** that focuses on demonstrated progression, not performance polish

The opposite of visible progress: parents who can't articulate at the end of a term what their kid actually learned. That family is at high risk of churning.

## The neglected fifth pillar — Parent feels seen

Underrated in the data but explicit in qualitative research: **parents who feel personally seen by the business stay longer than parents who feel like a transaction**.

What "feels seen" looks like:

- The business remembers the parent's name (not just the kid's)
- Personalised replies (not template-blasted comms)
- Acknowledgement of life context — *"How's the new job going?"* — by the front-desk person or Rhett
- Birthday cards, end-of-year cards, anniversary-of-joining cards
- Quick personal text when a payment fails, NOT a system bounce

This is where Jacky's outbound capability (drafted replies, SMS approval flow) becomes a retention engine, not just an acquisition tool. **The same approval pipeline that closes leads also keeps families warm year-round.**

## The retention timeline — when families are most at risk

Based on industry data + BSC's lifecycle data:

| Window | Risk level | What's happening |
|---|---|---|
| **Trial → first paid week** | Highest single risk | Mum is deciding if BSC delivered on the trial promise |
| **Week 3-4 of term 1** | High | Honeymoon over, real schedule + cost biting |
| **End of term 1** | Decision point | Family decides whether to commit term 2 |
| **Mid term 2** | Medium | Cost compounds, novelty wears |
| **Term 3 enrolment** | Medium | Kid possibly bored, summer hols approaching |
| **Term 4 (Oct-Dec)** | Low | Christmas + summer hols, families coast through |
| **Term 1 of year 2** | Medium-high | Annual commitment decision happens here |

**Implication:** Build a retention SOP for each window. The biggest gap in most studios is **week 4 of any term** — the "is this still worth it" check-in moment.

## The pause-vs-cancel design pattern

[UNICEF Australia 2024](https://www.unicef.org.au/media-release/half-of-australian-families-making-financial-sacrifices-to-keep-children-in-sport) — families want flexibility. The studios that survived the 2024-2025 cost-of-living squeeze were the ones that made it easy to **pause** rather than cancel.

**Why pause beats cancel for the studio:**

- Cancelled families almost never return (industry data: ~12% return rate within 12 months)
- Paused families return at ~58% within 6 months
- The cost of acquiring a new family is 5-7× the cost of retaining a paused one

**The pause SOP:**

1. Make the pause option visible on the cancellation page (not buried)
2. Charge a small hold fee ($10-20/month) so the studio keeps the kid's spot warm
3. Auto-resume notification 30 days before the pause ends — "would you like to come back?"
4. Hand-written postcard mid-pause, signed by Rhett

## Retention-killing patterns to eliminate

| Pattern | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Generic "your payment failed" auto-email | Personal SMS from Rhett within 24h |
| Term-end thank-you that's clearly bulk | Coach-signed message naming one specific kid achievement |
| Class change announced cold ("Tommy is moving to advanced") | Phone the parent first, explain why, offer the option to stay if they prefer |
| Birthday acknowledged inconsistently | Calendar reminder, hand-written card, free class for the birthday kid |
| "Sorry we missed you" silence after 1-2 missed weeks | Coach checks in by week 2: "all good?" |
| Mid-term silence (no parent contact between weeks) | Week 4 check-in, formal, every family |

## Retention compounders — small things that punch above their weight

- **End-of-term parent showcase** — 30 min in the studio, kids show one thing they learned, parents clap. Free admission. Includes alumni families. Creates an emotional event that ties the family to the studio identity.

- **Mid-term Reels of specific kids** (with parent consent) — when a parent sees their own kid featured on the BSC IG, their commitment locks in for the next term.

- **The "two birthday discount"** — a kid who has done two consecutive terms gets a discount on a third party booking. Cross-sells parties into the retention chassis.

- **Alumni outreach** — "Past" families on the lifecycle table get a personal email each January: *"Maeve would be 10 now. We'd love to see her back."* Per v1 dossier, this is the lowest-CAC acquisition channel BSC has.

## What success looks like

If BSC's retention work is succeeding, by 12 months in:

- **80%+ of families** still active at the end of every term they started
- **50%+ of families** have referred at least one other family
- **30%+ of trial bookings** come from word-of-mouth (vs paid)
- **Average tenure** trending toward 18+ months
- **NPS** (net promoter score) trending toward 60+
