# 09 · Children's Activity Industry Trends (2026)

*Three tailwinds, two headwinds. What's changing under BSC's feet, and what's working with us.*

## The macro picture

In 2026, three structural forces are simultaneously pushing parents *toward* the kind of activity BSC sells, while two forces are working against the broader kids' activity sector. Knowing which is which is the difference between strategy and noise.

## 🟢 Tailwind 1 — The under-16 social media ban (Dec 2025)

Australia's [under-16 social media ban](https://www.esafety.gov.au/parents/issues-and-advice/screen-time) took effect **10 December 2025**. For the first time, the government has formally endorsed the "screens are harmful" narrative. Parents who once accepted gaming and TikTok as the default after-school activity have **lost their main excuse**.

What this does to the market:
- Parents are *actively shopping* for replacement activities for kids who lost screen time
- The "screen antidote" pitch is now politically and culturally validated, not eccentric
- News coverage of the ban has been continuous since Dec 2025 → parents are primed

**BSC angle:** Lead with the screen-free reality in content. Don't argue. Don't preach. Just show the kid coming OFF the apparatus glowing, with no iPad in sight. The framing is *already in their head* — your content just has to confirm it.

---

## 🟢 Tailwind 2 — Blue Card expansion (Sept 2025)

The Queensland Blue Card system was [expanded on 20 September 2025](https://www.qld.gov.au/law/laws-regulated-industries-and-accountability/queensland-laws-and-regulations/regulated-industries-and-licensing/blue-card-services) to explicitly include entertainment and amusement categories. Two effects:

1. Parents are increasingly **aware** these checks exist — and checking
2. Activity providers operating outside the framework will increasingly stand out as risky

**BSC angle:** Make Blue Card status a prominent, visible signal on every coach bio page. Cost to do: zero. Signal value: high. Competitors who DON'T do this will become conspicuous over the next 12 months.

---

## 🟢 Tailwind 3 — Peer-reviewed evidence is mounting

The [Stevens et al. 2023 scoping review (PMC10003779)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10003779/) summarised 57 studies across 42 circus interventions. The University of South Australia [Cirkidz Tweenz study](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2019/05/190509101843.htm) documented significant lifts in stress relief, self-esteem, confidence, and socialisation across 55 kids aged 8–14. The UNSW research from Topic B in [AUDIENCE-DOSSIER-v2.md](../AUDIENCE-DOSSIER-v2.md) frames circus as 7× more effective than standard PE for child confidence.

What this does to the market:
- Circus training is no longer "alternative" — it's evidence-backed wellness
- BSC can cite peer-reviewed research in marketing without overclaiming
- NDIS-funded providers can use the same evidence to justify referrals

**BSC angle:** A single "What the research says about circus + kids' confidence" page on the BSC website, citing 3-4 of the peer-reviewed studies, becomes a credibility moat that competitors (especially national chains and standard sport clubs) literally cannot copy.

## 🟢 Tailwind 4 (bonus) — NDIS / Thriving Kids transition

The Thriving Kids program launches **1 October 2026**, replacing parts of the NDIS for kids 0–8 with developmental delay or autism (low-to-moderate needs). Provider procurement runs through 2026. Per the [UWA April 2026 article](https://www.uwa.edu.au/news/article/2026/april/autism-diagnoses-are-up-largely-fuelled-by-ndis-what-happens-next-isnt-clear), autism diagnoses are rising fast and parents of these kids are actively shopping for inclusive providers.

**BSC angle:** Register as a Thriving Kids community provider this year. This is the single biggest 6-month opportunity in this dossier (per v1 + v2). Once on the list, state-funded families can attend BSC without needing an NDIS plan.

## 🔴 Headwind 1 — Cost of living

[Triple P 2024](https://thesector.com.au/2024/05/07/triple-ps-2024-national-parenting-survey-reveals-rising-stress-emotion-and-exhaustion/) — 9 in 10 parents have reduced spending. [UNICEF Australia 2024](https://www.unicef.org.au/media-release/half-of-australian-families-making-financial-sacrifices-to-keep-children-in-sport) — 2 in 5 households considering withdrawing kids from sport. The macro reality is: kids' activity budgets are being squeezed.

**Implications for BSC:**
- Don't compete on price — but DO make price transparent and offer flexibility (pause, sibling discount, voucher)
- The families most exposed to cost pressure are the families MOST motivated by Play On voucher
- A premium-priced offer with strong outcomes can still win — provided the value proposition is unambiguous

**BSC angle:** Lead with outcomes ("confidence in 12 weeks") not features ("4 disciplines"). Cost-anxious parents reject feature-led pitches.

---

## 🔴 Headwind 2 — Time poverty (cited as the #1 leave reason)

[Allianz 2024](https://www.allianz.com.au/about-us/media-hub/aussie-kids-drop-out-of-sport.html) — **54% of parents cite time commitment as the biggest barrier** to kids' activities. This is *the* #1 reason families drop out of all activities — bigger than cost.

**Implications for BSC:**
- Drive time matters → Coomera/Pimpama families dropping is a real risk after the honeymoon period
- Cluster siblings in the same hour to compress the parent's commitment
- Saturday morning slots are unusually high-value because they're family-time-friendly

**BSC angle:** Build the customer journey (doc 15) around making the *parent's* week simpler, not just the kid's. The parent is the customer.

## Macro market direction (where is the kids' activity sector going?)

### Diversifying revenue, away from "weekly term fees" only

Per [HiSawyer's research on children's activity revenue diversification](https://www.hisawyer.com/for-business/blog/how-to-diversify-revenue-for-childrens-activity-businesses), the sector is shifting toward multi-revenue-stream models:

- **Termly classes** as the core
- **Holiday programmes** as paid acquisition funnels into termly
- **Birthday parties** as high-margin events that also feed termly enrolment
- **Camps + intensives** as premium add-ons for committed families
- **Merchandise + photos** as low-effort margin

BSC already operates this way structurally — the lever is execution quality on each stream.

### Pivot from skill to wellbeing positioning

The historical sector positioning was "we'll teach your kid to do X." The 2026 positioning that's winning is "we'll help your kid become Y." Skill is the means; wellbeing is the end. This is consistent with the AIHW + Headspace + Triple P backdrop of elevated child mental-health concern.

### Local-first beats franchise

BOUNCE and GKR Karate are the GC's biggest national chains and are *not* growing share in 2026 — local operators with strong identity (Hartmann Taekwondo, Gold Medal Gymnastics) are. Parents in 2026 prefer specific-local over generic-national. BSC is local-first by default.

### UGC content + selfie-mode replaces polished commercial

Documented in **doc 05** (Instagram trends) and **doc 04** (Facebook trends): the polished-commercial era is over. Authentic, vertical, native content beats production at 8.7× engagement.

### Direct messaging becomes the primary acquisition channel

DM → first enquiry → trial booking is replacing form-submission flows. Parents are increasingly hesitant to fill in long forms. Instagram DMs and Facebook Messenger to a business are now the dominant first-touch in the family/parent niche.

**Implication:** BSC's automated DM flow (Jacky drafts → Rhett approves) is exactly the right system for this moment. Most competitors are still relying on website forms.

## Risks BSC should watch for in 2026-2027

| Risk | Probability | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| New circus competitor opens on Gold Coast | Low (high barrier — equipment + coaches) | Lock in homeschool + NDIS audiences fast; first-mover advantage |
| Flipside opens a GC branch | Medium | Compete on locality, price, and Saturday slots |
| Meta further restricts under-18 ad targeting | High | Already happened; doc 04's playbook is the response |
| Play On vouchers cancelled | Low | Don't build the business on vouchers alone |
| Major safeguarding incident in the sector | Low but high-impact | Over-invest in Blue Card visibility before it becomes an issue |
| Cost of living gets worse | High | Pause/hold-fee + sibling discount + transparent pricing |

## Risks that DON'T matter (don't waste cycles on these)

- A national chain entering the GC circus market (no commercial logic)
- An algorithm change that "kills" Reels (algorithm risk is symmetric across competitors)
- A theme park or play centre adding "circus classes" as an upsell (lacks credibility)
