# 12 · Confidence-Building Activity Research

*The peer-reviewed case for circus as a child-confidence intervention. Use this doc to back any BSC claim about confidence outcomes.*

## The headline study

The most important single source: **Stevens et al. 2023 — "Circus Activities as a Health Intervention: A Scoping Review"**, [PubMed Central PMC10003779](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10003779/).

The review synthesised **57 sources** covering **42 distinct circus interventions**. Findings:

| Outcome dimension | Studies reporting positive effect |
|---|---|
| **Confidence / self-esteem** | **23 studies** |
| **Social skills** | **21 studies** |
| **Resilience** | **8 studies** |
| **Inclusion / belonging** | **7 studies** |

Five studies in the review focused specifically on **autistic and neurodiverse children**, with participants aged 3–13+. Documented outcomes for this subgroup:

- Teamwork
- Sociability
- Emotional control
- Motor competence
- Postural control

This is the **peer-reviewed credibility moat** for BSC's neurodivergent-welcoming positioning. No competitor on the Gold Coast is citing this research because none of them are positioned as inclusive-by-design.

## The mechanism — why circus works

The Stevens et al. review explains the mechanism using [Self-Determination Theory](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/). Three innate psychological needs must be satisfied for human well-being:

| Need | How circus satisfies it |
|---|---|
| **Autonomy** | Kids choose their apparatus, choose how high to climb, choose their performance level |
| **Competence** | Progressive challenge — every skill has a "next step up" that's achievable |
| **Relatedness** | Group classes, partner work, end-of-term performance to community |

The review notes that progress in circus is *"inherently satisfying and highly motivating"* because the progressive challenge structure lets each child progress at their own pace and experience **early, frequent wins** — vs sport, where structured competition creates winners and losers from week 1.

## The University of South Australia study

Dr Richard McGrath of UniSA studied 55 participants aged 8–14 in the Cirkidz Tweenz program ([source via ScienceDaily 2019](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2019/05/190509101843.htm)). Significant improvements were measured across **four domains**:

1. **Stress relief**
2. **Self-esteem**
3. **Confidence**
4. **Socialisation**

This is a real Australian study with a measurable sample size. It's quotable in marketing materials.

## The UNSW comparison study

Referenced in [AUDIENCE-DOSSIER-v2.md](../AUDIENCE-DOSSIER-v2.md): UNSW research found circus training boosted confidence and emotional regulation **7× more effectively than standard PE**. (Direct primary source via NIH search; v2 cites the [NIH scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10003779/) which references this finding.)

The "7× more effective" stat is striking but should be cited carefully. Use as a directional claim ("circus is significantly more effective than standard PE") in marketing copy, and link to the peer-reviewed source if asked.

## Why circus works for SHY kids specifically

Three structural features of circus training that suit anxious/shy children:

1. **No "win condition"** — there's no goal that's reached by being faster, stronger, or beating someone. The kid is competing with their previous self, not with peers.
2. **Solo apparatus options** — silks, lyra, partner-acro all allow the kid to work in pairs OR alone, depending on their comfort.
3. **Performance is optional** — end-of-term showcases are opt-in. Backstage roles exist. A shy kid can be a full participant without ever being publicly on stage.

Compare with team sports: every match exposes the kid publicly. Compare with elite gymnastics: the squad ranks publicly. Compare with dance recitals: the choreography is a public test. Circus is uniquely well-suited to the kid who needs **early wins without public scrutiny**.

## Why circus works for NEURODIVERSE kids specifically

The PMC review's five-study subgroup on autism/dev-delay kids documented outcomes across teamwork, sociability, emotional control, motor competence, postural control. Why these specifically?

| Outcome | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Motor competence** | Apparatus work demands proprioceptive feedback — autistic kids often have under-developed proprioception and find circus genuinely helpful |
| **Emotional control** | Apparatus work creates manageable stress that the kid learns to regulate in real time |
| **Postural control** | Vestibular system (balance) often atypical in autism; circus directly trains it |
| **Sociability** | Highly-structured class environment with predictable routines suits autistic kids more than open-ended play |
| **Teamwork** | Partner-acro creates *necessary* physical cooperation with another kid, which feels different from social cooperation |

This is why BSC's "Half our families are NDIS or Thriving Kids" line isn't just inclusive theatre — it's an evidence-backed product fit.

- ❌ Do NOT claim circus "cures" anxiety, ADHD, autism, or any disorder
- ❌ Do NOT claim BSC is "the" research-backed option
- ✅ DO claim circus is "an" evidence-backed practice for confidence + social-skill outcomes
- ✅ DO link to the peer-reviewed sources

## What other activities do (comparison)

To position circus's evidence base against alternatives, here's a brief honest reality:

| Activity | Confidence evidence base |
|---|---|
| **Circus** | Strong (PMC review, UniSA study, NIH-backed research) |
| Team sport (soccer, netball) | Mixed — confidence boost for kids who succeed, deficit for kids who don't |
| Dance | Some evidence for confidence + self-image in girls; thinner for boys |
| Martial arts | Strong evidence for discipline and respect; less direct on confidence |
| Drama | Strong for verbal confidence and presentation skills; less for physical |
| Gymnastics | Mixed — competitive squad models can erode confidence in middle-tier kids |

BSC's combination — circus + multi-discipline + non-competitive — sits at the very top of this comparison.
