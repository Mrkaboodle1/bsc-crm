# 11 · Screen Time Concern Research

*Screen-time anxiety is the single biggest cultural backdrop to BSC's market in 2026. This doc shows the data, the language, and how to use both.*

## The numbers

### Australian kids' screen-time exposure

- **~90% of Australian kids look at screens each week**, most for 10+ hours per week — [ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/articles/kids-clock-10-or-more-hours-screen-time-week)
- **91.8% of children aged 2–4 exceed the 1-hour-per-day national guideline** — [PMC8845031](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8845031/)
- **11.4% of children aged 5–12 exceed the 2-hour-per-day guideline** (note: this is the percentage exceeding by a meaningful margin; many more sit at the guideline) — same PMC source

The compound picture: every parent of an Australian kid 2–12 is operating with a kid who's getting more screen time than national guidelines suggest. **This is not a marginal anxiety. It's a mass anxiety.**

### Parental guilt around screens

The 2025 nature/screen-balance research ([PMC12752391](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12752391/)) found parents describe their children's immersive offline play as **"relief from screen-related parenting challenges and associated guilt"**.

In simpler terms: parents feel **guilty** about their kids' screen use, and they describe activities that physically displace screens as **guilt-relievers**.

### Government policy backdrop

On **10 December 2025**, Australia's under-16 social media ban took effect — see the [eSafety screen-time guidance](https://www.esafety.gov.au/parents/issues-and-advice/screen-time). For the first time, the federal government has formally endorsed the framing that screens are harmful to under-16s.

**Why this matters commercially:** Parents who were previously embarrassed about being "anti-screen" now have **state-level cover** to pull their kids off devices. The activity providers who can credibly position as "screen antidote" pick up the displaced hours.

### Cross-data: kids quitting sport TO play more screens

[AusPlay / Allianz](https://www.allianz.com.au/about-us/media-hub/aussie-kids-drop-out-of-sport.html) — **30% of kids who quit out-of-school sport did so to prioritise gaming and other screen-based activities**. This is a real reverse-pressure: the wrong sort of activity (long drives, boring weeks, no friends) actively LOSES to gaming.

**Implication for BSC:** The activity has to be *more compelling than the iPad*. That sounds obvious but it's a higher bar than most operators design for.

## The language parents use about screens

From mum-group threads, school P&C minutes, and the qualitative side of the PMC research:

**Said publicly (acceptable):**
- *"We've cut back on screens this year."*
- *"He's only allowed an hour on weekends."*
- *"We try to do something active every day."*

**Said privately (real):**
- *"He'd be on the iPad 12 hours a day if I let him."*
- *"I don't even know how to fight it anymore."*
- *"I gave up. I just hide it after dinner."*
- *"I feel like a bad parent every single day."*

**What BSC should NOT say:**

- ❌ *"Reduce screen time with our classes!"* (preachy, makes the parent feel judged)
- ❌ *"Parents — your kids are addicted to screens!"* (alarmist, sells fear not relief)
- ❌ *"Phones are ruining childhood!"* (political; alienates half the audience)

**What BSC SHOULD say:**

- ✅ *"60 minutes off the iPad. Watch what happens."*
- ✅ *"He won't ask for the iPad on the drive home."* (specific, real)
- ✅ *"Screens make her quiet. Circus makes her noisy in all the right ways."*

The pattern: **describe the outcome you want the mum to feel proud of**, not the problem you're solving.

## How to weave screens into BSC content

### Content angle 1 — Post-class glow Reel

Wide shot of a kid coming OFF the silks, sweaty, exhausted, grinning. No iPad visible. Mum filming from the bench. Caption: *"This is what 60 minutes off the iPad looks like."*

### Content angle 2 — Coach intro

*"I'm Sarah. I've been teaching circus for 9 years. The thing every parent tells me after term 1: 'She doesn't ask for the iPad on Saturday mornings anymore. She's too excited about Saturday.'"*

### Content angle 3 — Parent testimonial

> *"I used to fight him on screens after school. Now he comes home from circus and wants to PRACTICE in the lounge room. Last Tuesday I caught him doing a forward roll over the dog. I'm not even mad."*

### Content angle 4 — Story carousel

3-slide IG carousel:

- Slide 1: Photo of kid on iPad, slightly slumped
- Slide 2: Photo of same kid on silks, beaming
- Slide 3: Text — *"Two hours. Same kid. Same Saturday."*

## Audience segments that respond most strongly

### High-resonance: parents of boys 6-12

Boys' screen exposure trends higher than girls', and parental anxiety is louder in this segment. Gaming-quit-from-sport data (30%) skews male. **Direct creative at boy-parents** explicitly: *"Is your son's iPad time creeping?"*

### High-resonance: parents of neurodiverse kids

Per the AIHW data, ADHD is the #1 disorder in boys (7.4%). Parents of kids with ADHD or autism are often even more sensitised to screen overuse and more actively seeking high-stimulation alternatives. BSC's neurodivergent-welcoming positioning compounds with the screen angle here.

### Medium-resonance: parents of girls 8-12

Girls' screen time skews toward social media and parasocial content (YouTube creators, TikTok, FaceTime with friends). The under-16 social media ban hits this segment hardest. The angle here is different: *"What does she do after school now that TikTok's gone?"*

### Lower-resonance: parents of under-5s

This cohort over-indexes on screen exposure (91.8% exceed guidelines) but the parental conversation hasn't fully caught up — under-5 parents are more accepting of educational/distraction screen use. Don't lead with screen-time angle for toddler classes; lead with developmental angles (motor skills, social play).

## Tactical content cadence

If BSC produces 12 pieces of content per month (3 Reels/week, doc 05):

- **2-3 should explicitly reference screens** — direct content
- **4-5 should implicitly show screen-free joy** — kids glowing, no devices visible
- **The rest** can address other themes (confidence, social, fitness, etc.)

Over-doing the screen angle ("look how much we hate screens!") will read preachy. The 2-3 explicit pieces are the message; the 4-5 implicit pieces are the proof.

## A note on the social media ban for under-16s

If BSC posts content directly featuring kids under 16 (which it must — that's the product), be aware:

- Photo/video consent is now a **legal grey area** in addition to a parental preference
- Best practice in 2026: explicit written consent for any individual identifiable kid, with the kid's first name only (never surname), and **opt-out by default** for under-13s
- The eSafety Commissioner's guidance is the canonical source: [esafety.gov.au/parents/issues-and-advice](https://www.esafety.gov.au/parents/issues-and-advice/screen-time)

BSC's existing rule ("Never identify a child by name in public-facing copy unless photo consent is on file") is exactly right and should be enforced more strictly in 2026 than 2024.
